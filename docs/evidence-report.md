The Hakaluki 27 Sep check compares a median NISAR baseline dated 29 Jun with HLS 4 Jul, and NISAR 27 Sep with HLS 24 Sep. Temporal offsets, cloud masking, mixed 30 m cells, and MNDWI thresholds limit the comparison. HLS is a useful cross-sensor check, not field truth.

NASA’s [Mozambique NISAR visualization](https://svs.gsfc.nasa.gov/5661/) identifies the 5 and 17 Jan L-band observations and shows before/during flood imagery over Chókwè and the river basins. The project separately processed those exact two GCOV BETA scenes and published run `ec8fa5ee15ae41d1aa89f930f2b47e36` as version `2026.10.29-chokwe`. Its single-date baseline limits temporal characterization. The HLS comparison selected S30 tile T36JVT on 7 and 22 Jan, but the 1.26% common-clear fraction leaves all radar candidate area unassessed; the saved 0.0 F1 is therefore not a meaningful accuracy estimate and is suppressed by the coverage gate. [CIMA’s 16 Jan map](https://www.cimafoundation.org/en/news/mozambique-heavy-rainfall-in-the-south-cooperation-and-monitoring-to-manage-flood-risk/) uses Copernicus Sentinel-1/2 and labels permanent water, flooded areas, and no-data. [UNOSAT’s 23–24 Jan product](https://unosat.org/products/4232) provides a later independent Sentinel-1 water-extent layer. Rasterized on the native NISAR grid, it overlaps 88.2% and 89.1% of candidates on 23 and 24 Jan, respectively; all candidates are inside the product's analysis extent. This is useful positive spatial support, but the 6–7 day offset, product's preliminary status, lack of field validation, and absence of reference-negative labels mean it is not an accuracy assessment or proof that every candidate was floodwater. See [the overlap details](chokwe-unosat-overlap.json).

## Forest case

The Rondônia study uses seven real NISAR PROVISIONAL GCOV dates and method `forest-hv-persistence-1.0.0`. Its 29 Sep frame contains one **0.0128 km²** persistent radar-change candidate. HLS has 97.45% common-clear coverage for the selected pair, and 71.1% of this small candidate overlaps the exploratory joint NDVI/NBR-decrease signature. This is positive optical context, not a forest-loss reference classification: there are no assessed negative labels, so precision, recall, and F1 remain null. The map now offers a GLAD-S2 alert overlay filtered to the selected NISAR study window. A dated GLAD alert overlapping the candidate would be independent positive event support, but would not establish permanence or supply reference-negative labels; absence remains unassessed. The boundary is an analyst-defined rectangle, and the area is not a deforestation estimate.

## Volcano case

The Hayli Gubbi/Erta Ale package contains three official NISAR L2 GUNW BETA intervals and two ARIA Sentinel-1 GUNW comparison intervals. It displays apparent line-of-sight phase-derived change, coherence, and quality screens. Sentinel-1 has different dates and viewing directions and is complementary context, not deformation ground truth. The reference patch is assumed stable, not surveyed. The Smithsonian/USGS record establishes eruption context; it does not attribute each measured radar feature to the eruption. No eruption prediction or confirmed-causation claim is made.

## Reporting rules

- A completed processing run is not a validated interpretation.
- The 20% common-clear threshold determines whether the wetland optical comparison can be assessed; passing it does not establish accuracy.
- Preserve zeros, NoData, clouds, and unassessed regions as distinct states. Do not count unassessed pixels as reference negatives.
- Do not tune thresholds against the held-out reference or choose dates by agreement score.
- The global map shows imagery, catalog locations, and scene footprints. Only published study areas have project-derived results; blank areas mean **no result available**, not “no change.”
- Report supported change candidates with their dates, footprint, method, coverage, independent evidence, and uncertainty. Keep an event-level reference separate from a local algorithm score.
